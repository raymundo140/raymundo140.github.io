## Overview

At the CNRS-AIST Joint Robotics Laboratory in Tsukuba, I worked on autonomous mobile manipulation using CALL-M, a research mobile manipulator built for operation in human-centered indoor environments.

My work centered on two research projects:

- Autonomous mapping and exploration in unknown, dynamic environments.
- Autonomous door opening through integrated perception, navigation, and manipulation.

Across both projects, I worked on ROS 2 architecture, LiDAR/RGB-D perception, point clouds, TF, SLAM, frontier exploration, Nav2, dynamic-obstacle filtering, mobile-base control, manipulation, distributed execution, real-robot testing, failure analysis, and research writing.

## CALL-M

CALL-M combines:

- TriOrb omnidirectional mobile base.
- UR5e manipulator.
- Robotiq 2F-85 gripper.
- RPLIDAR sensors.
- ZED Mini stereo cameras.
- Intel RealSense D435i.
- Onboard Intel and NVIDIA compute.

## Project 1 — Autonomous Mapping

I developed a modular ROS 2 architecture that lets CALL-M autonomously explore an unknown environment, build an occupancy map, supervise navigation goals, recover from failures, filter moving objects, and stop when exploration is complete.

My main contributions included:

- Multimodal range-sensor integration.
- Frontier extraction and target evaluation.
- Nav2 goal dispatch and supervision.
- Timeout and failure recovery.
- Spatial memory of failed targets.
- Mission-progress tracking.
- Mission-completion logic.
- Static–dynamic obstacle separation.
- Object clustering, tracking, and velocity estimation.
- Simulation and physical-robot validation.

The exploration architecture completed 10/10 simulated missions compared with 7/10 for a nearest-frontier baseline, and all 5/5 physical mapping sessions reached autonomous completion.

## Project 2 — Autonomous Door Opening

I also worked on a mobile-manipulation pipeline for autonomous door opening. The system:

- Detects a door using machine-learning-based perception.
- Approaches it using Nav2.
- Localizes the handle in 3D from RGB-D data.
- Transforms the target through ROS 2 TF.
- Aligns the omnidirectional base.
- Moves the UR5e to a pre-grasp pose.
- Grasps and actuates the handle.
- Executes the door-opening motion.

The pipeline was tested in 20 real-robot trials. In the 10 nominal trials, perception, navigation, 3D localization, alignment, and pre-grasp each succeeded 10/10; complete door opening succeeded 8/10. The experiments identified physical contact and manipulation geometry as the main remaining limitations.
