## Robot Setup & Commissioning

A significant part of the role involved assembling, wiring, and commissioning robots from the ground up for deployment in production cells. This included:

### Electrical Wiring & Cabling
- Wired robot controllers, power supplies, and I/O systems according to electrical schematics
- Performed cable management for robot arms, control cabinets, and peripheral devices
- Routed and secured wiring for end-effectors, sensors, and communication interfaces
- Diagnosed and resolved electrical faults including wiring errors, connector failures, and signal issues

### Mechanical Assembly & Integration
- Physically assembled robot arms, mounting structures, and tool interfaces
- Integrated robots into production cell workstations and worktables
- Installed and aligned robot bases and end-of-arm tooling for pick-and-place operations
- Fabricated and installed custom mechanical components including brackets, mounts, tool holders, fixtures, and robot add-ons such as onboard computer enclosures and storage systems for robot-mounted electronics. All designed in SolidWorks and manufactured via 3D printing and laser cutting

### Calibration & Validation
- Performed joint calibration and tool center point (TCP) setup on UR and xArm systems
- Validated robot reach, repeatability, and cycle performance within production cell layouts
- Through systematic commissioning and maintenance procedures, robot downtime was reduced by **20%** across the production floor

## End-Effector Design: Custom Pick-and-Place Gripper

One of the main engineering projects was the full design, fabrication, and validation of a custom high-precision gripper for pick-and-place tasks, mounted on an industrial robot arm.

### Design Process (SolidWorks)
- Modeled the complete gripper geometry, finger profiles, and mechanical assembly in **SolidWorks**
- Designed for target object geometry, weight, and surface properties to ensure reliable grasping
- Iterated through multiple design revisions, refining finger geometry, actuation clearances, and mounting interface with each cycle

### Fabrication
- **3D printing** — manufactured finger bodies, structural components, and prototype parts for rapid iteration
- **Laser cutting** — precision-cut structural plates and reinforcement elements for the final assembly

### Validation & Results
- Conducted production-cell testing to validate grasp reliability, cycle repeatability, and throughput
- Achieved a **30% increase in production throughput** over the previous solution
- Demonstrated consistent handling repeatability across extended production cycles

## Camera System Installation: Multi-Purpose Robot Vision Infrastructure

Beyond robot setup and mechanical work, I installed and commissioned a **USB camera tracking system** for the lab, a reusable vision infrastructure built for current and future robotics projects.

### Scope & Purpose
The system was designed as a **multi-purpose platform** capable of:
- **Robot position and movement tracking** — monitoring arm trajectories and workspace activity
- **Object detection** — identifying parts, tools, and items within the robot workspace
- **People detection** — safety monitoring and human presence awareness in the lab environment

### Implementation
- Selected camera placement to maximize workspace coverage for robot monitoring
- Physically mounted and secured USB cameras at optimal angles and positions
- Wired camera connections and managed cable routing throughout the lab
- Configured camera feeds and verified image quality and coverage zones

### Value
By building a general-purpose vision infrastructure rather than a single-use system, the installation serves as a reusable platform that future robotics projects at the lab can leverage for tracking, detection, and perception tasks.

## Custom Parts Fabrication for Warehouse Robotics

Throughout the internship, I designed and fabricated a range of **custom mechanical parts** used across the lab's robot systems and warehouse automation setups:

- **Onboard computer & electronics mounts** — custom storage enclosures and mounting systems designed to house onboard computers and electronic components directly on the robot body, keeping hardware organized, protected, and within the robot's workspace
- **Mounting brackets** — robot arm mounts and base fixtures for stable installation
- **Tool holders** — end-of-arm tooling interfaces and quick-change adapters
- **Structural fixtures** — frames and supports for integrating robots into warehouse workflows
- **Custom enclosures and covers** — protective and organizational components for robot wiring and electronics

All parts were designed in **SolidWorks**, manufactured via **3D printing** and **laser cutting**, and validated through physical fit-testing before deployment on the robots.
