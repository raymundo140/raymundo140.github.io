## Overview

At the CNRS-AIST Joint Robotics Laboratory, I developed a modular ROS 2 system for autonomous mapping with CALL-M, an omnidirectional mobile manipulator. The system turns mapping into a supervised autonomy problem: it fuses range sensing, builds an occupancy map, identifies where to explore next, sends and monitors navigation goals, recovers from failures, handles moving obstacles, tracks mission progress, and decides when exploration is complete.

## System Architecture

The mapping stack combines multimodal scan fusion, static–dynamic obstacle filtering, SLAM Toolbox, frontier-based exploration, and Nav2. Sensor observations from LiDAR and RGB-D/stereo cameras are transformed into a consistent planar representation used by mapping and navigation.

CALL-M distributes computation across onboard computers and an operator workstation through ROS 2, allowing perception, mapping, exploration, and navigation components to run as independent modules.

## My Contribution — Exploration Management

I developed a reusable exploration-management framework that extends frontier exploration beyond "find the nearest unknown area and go there." The framework:

- Extracts and clusters frontier regions.
- Evaluates candidate targets.
- Sends goals to Nav2.
- Supervises active navigation actions.
- Detects timeouts and failures.
- Stores spatial memory of failed targets.
- Temporarily excludes problematic regions.
- Re-selects targets after failed attempts.
- Tracks exploration progress.
- Determines mission completion.
- Prevents conflicting goal requests from asynchronous callbacks.

This supervisory layer made exploration more reliable during long autonomous runs and reduced repeated navigation attempts toward low-value or unreachable targets.

I also developed a ROS 2 framework that separates persistent environmental structure from moving observations before they reach SLAM. The filter processes incoming LaserScan data, builds short-term spatial evidence, separates static and dynamic observations, clusters moving measurements, tracks objects across time, and estimates obstacle velocity, producing separate outputs for mapping and navigation. Static observations are used for persistent map construction; dynamic observations remain available to the navigation stack for collision avoidance. The goal is not to hide people from the robot — it is to keep people from becoming walls in the map.

## Results

**Simulation:**
- 10/10 autonomous mission completions with the supervised architecture.
- 7/10 completions for the nearest-frontier baseline.
- 95.3 ± 2.4% reachable-space coverage.
- Navigation goals reduced from 161.9 to 56.1 on average.
- Progress per goal increased from 1.21 m to 3.86 m.
- Frontier evaluations reduced from 174.3 to 67.6 per mission.

**Real robot:**
- 5/5 autonomous mapping sessions reached completion.
- 4.90 m measured room dimension reconstructed as 4.85 ± 0.05 m.
- 6.80 m measured dimension reconstructed as 7.13 ± 0.07 m.
