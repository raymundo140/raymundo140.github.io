## Overview

At AIST, I worked on an end-to-end mobile-manipulation pipeline that enables CALL-M to find a door, approach it, localize the handle in 3D, align the base, position the arm, grasp and actuate the handle, and open the door. The project connects perception, navigation, coordinate transforms, mobile-base control, manipulation, and physical contact in a single ROS 2 system.

## Pipeline

After manual initial pose setup in RViz, the system executes seven stages:

1. Door Search
2. Approach
3. Handle Detection
4. 3D Estimation
5. Fine Alignment
6. Pre-Grasp
7. Contact, Grasp, Handle Actuation, and Door Opening

## Perception, Navigation, and Alignment

A YOLO-based detector identifies the door while CALL-M searches the environment. RGB-D sensing is then used to estimate the handle position in 3D. ROS 2 TF transforms convert that target between camera, mobile-base, manipulator, and map frames.

Nav2 brings the robot to an approach position in front of the door. The omnidirectional TriOrb base then performs finer corrections so that the handle falls inside the manipulator's reachable workspace.

## My Contribution — Manipulation

The UR5e moves to a pre-grasp pose based on the estimated handle location. The gripper advances, closes around the lever, actuates the latch, and executes the opening motion.

The physical interaction currently follows a predefined Cartesian sequence, which makes contact geometry and residual alignment error important sources of failure.

## Results

The system was evaluated in 20 real-robot trials.

**Nominal condition:**
- Door Search: 10/10
- Approach: 10/10
- Handle Detection: 10/10
- 3D Estimation: 10/10
- Fine Alignment: 10/10
- Pre-Grasp: 10/10
- Complete door opening: 8/10

Additional tests varied initial robot-to-door distance and initial end-effector height.

## Key Finding

The experiments showed that perception, localization, and navigation were more repeatable than the final physical interaction. The dominant failure modes were residual alignment error, grasp depth, force-limit activation, and insufficient latch displacement.

Future improvements include closed-loop pose correction, adaptive grasp planning, and force-aware manipulation.
