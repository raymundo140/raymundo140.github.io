## Overview

This project was developed at the Beijing Institute of Technology as a scale prototype of an IoT-enabled precision-agriculture system. The objective was to demonstrate how continuous environmental sensing, edge computing, automated actuation, and web-based monitoring can be integrated into a single platform to support data-driven agricultural management.

## System Architecture

A Raspberry Pi served as the central computing and coordination unit. Multiple environmental sensors were integrated around the scale agricultural environment to continuously collect information describing current growing conditions, with readings aggregated by the Raspberry Pi and used as inputs to the system's monitoring and automation logic.

Rather than functioning only as a passive monitoring station, the platform incorporated multiple actuators capable of responding to changing environmental conditions. Based on the sensed state of the agricultural environment, the system could activate the appropriate physical response when intervention was required — for example, triggering irrigation when additional water was needed, or activating ventilation when airflow was required.

## Closed-Loop Automation

This created a closed-loop IoT architecture in which the physical environment was continuously observed, environmental data was processed by the computing platform, and actuator commands were generated according to the needs detected by the system: sense, process, decide, actuate, and monitor.

A user-facing website was also developed as part of the platform, providing real-time visibility into the environmental information collected by the sensor network and allowing the user to remotely observe current agricultural conditions from a centralized dashboard. This connected the physical agricultural prototype with a digital monitoring layer, demonstrating how IoT systems can make distributed sensor information accessible to end users.

## Results

The complete project combined embedded/edge computing, environmental sensing, actuator control, IoT communication, automation logic, and web development into a single cyber-physical system. The scale prototype served as a proof of concept for how similar architectures could be expanded to monitor and automate larger agricultural environments, providing practical experience connecting hardware devices to a Raspberry Pi, processing live sensor data, controlling actuators from environmental conditions, and exposing physical-system data through a real-time web application.
